# Prima dată — money, the first time, done right

Oct 28, 2026 · @eddie

## In one screen

**Problem.** Romanians under 30 make their first money decisions alone, with the lowest financial knowledge in the EU (13% score high). The rules that shape their future are Romanian and change every year: where a salary's contributions go, a pension fund they never chose, inflation eating their deposits, 16% tax on gains from app-based brokers. Generic finance courses don't explain any of it, and nobody explains it at the moment it matters.

**Solution.** Prima dată ("the first time") is a Brilliant-style app where the exercises are your own life.

- Lessons arrive when a financial "first" happens: first payslip, first savings, first investing app, first freelance contract, first tax return.
- Each lesson uses your own numbers.
- Each lesson ends in one real action: a savings rule on payday, a decision about your pension fund, your tax figures for the 25 May deadline.

**Who pays.** Users never pay, and nobody can pay to be recommended.

- Banks, neobanks and brokers license the lessons inside their own apps, paying per active learner, because they need young customers who actually start saving and investing.
- Employers pay per seat to onboard their first-job hires.

**Verdict.** Projected **≈4.1/5** on the jury weights, up from 3.2 for the original "education app + referral fees" version. The redesign keeps your focus on the young generation and fixes the two things the jury would punish: "just presenting information" and a conflicted business model.

## The problem

The decisions that shape a Romanian's financial future start in their twenties, and the system makes them for you unless you understand it.

- **Knowledge.** 13% of Romanians have high financial knowledge, the lowest in the EU. 25% have no emergency savings (EU: 16%). Financial education in schools has been optional since a 2018 agreement between BNR, ASF, the Ministries of Education and Finance and the banking association, so most under-30s never had it.
- **The pension you never chose.** Employees under 35 must join a Pillar II fund within 4 months of being hired. Those who don't pick one are assigned a fund in proportion to market share. In 2012, 95.75% of new participants were assigned that way. About 4.75% of every gross salary goes there.
- **Inflation vs savings.** At July 2026's 8.2% inflation, 10,000 lei in a 4.5% deposit (after the 10% interest tax) lost about **384 lei** of purchasing power in a year. Even the top 7.6% tax-free Fidelis rate lost about 55 lei.
- **Investing apps and tax.** Since 2026, gains through a Romanian broker that withholds tax are taxed at 3% (held over a year) or 6%. Through a broker that doesn't withhold, typically a foreign app, the rate is 16% and you must file Declarația Unică by 25 May yourself. Investment income above 24,300 lei a year also triggers CASS (health contribution).
- **The rules move every year.** 2026 changed capital-gains rates, raised the minimum wage mid-year (thresholds didn't move) and pushed tens of thousands of young people into PFA contracts (+71% new PFAs in Q1).

The gap isn't access to information; banks, brokers and creators publish plenty. It's that nothing meets you at the moment you act, uses your own numbers, and helps you do the right thing then.

## Who it serves

**First user: the first 90 days of the first full-time job.** Maria, 23, a junior data analyst in Iași (synthetic persona).

- She earns 6,000 lei gross.
- She has Revolut and a broker app she opened after a TikTok video, and no savings.
- She doesn't know what Pillar II is, which fund she's in, or that her broker's gains may need a tax return.
- Her parents keep their money in deposits and tell her to buy an apartment.

The first job is the sharpest moment: the first payslip arrives, the pension fund deadline runs, and the first money with no parents watching lands in her account.

**Mass market.** About **2.0 million** Romanians aged 20–29 (Eurostat estimate, 1 Jan 2025: 1.02M aged 20–24, 0.99M aged 25–29). Then everyone up to 35 as new "firsts" arrive: a freelance contract, moving out, a first loan, a first home.

**Who pays is not the user.** Banks, neobanks and brokers fighting for these customers pay, and so do employers hiring them (see Business model). Two signals of how contested this segment is:

- Revolut has about 5 million retail customers in Romania (September 2026).
- Banca Transilvania launched the digital bank Salt Bank, which had \~500,000 clients after its first year and targets 1 million by the end of 2026.

## The product

**Four rules that make it more than an education app:**

1. **Triggered by a moment, not a syllabus.** A lesson opens when a "first" happens: payday, a balance building up, a broker deposit, a job offer, the tax deadline. No streaks for their own sake.
2. **Your numbers, not examples.** Every exercise runs on the user's payslip, balance or trades, typed in or photographed; with a licensed partner later, read from the bank.
3. **Predict, then see.** The Brilliant mechanic: guess first ("how much of your gross goes to your future?"), then a slider-driven reveal. 3–5 minutes per moment.
4. **Every moment ends in one action** the user chooses at their own bank or broker. Progress is counted in actions done, not quiz points.

**The moments library (Romania, 2026 rules):**

| Moment | Trigger | What you learn, with your own numbers | The action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First payslip | First salary lands, or a payslip photo | Where gross goes: 25% to pension (4.75% of gross into *your* Pillar II account), 10% health, 10% income tax; which fund you were assigned | Check your fund; set a "pay yourself first" rule for payday |
| First savings | A balance building up; the monthly inflation figure | Real return after the 10% tax and inflation; emergency buffer; how Tezaur and Fidelis work (monthly windows, 5,000 lei Fidelis minimum, tax-free) | A buffer target with an automatic transfer; the next state-bond window in your calendar |
| First investing app | First deposit or trade | 3%/6% withheld vs 16% self-declared; the 25 May deadline; the CASS threshold; fees; funds vs single stocks | Export your trades and get your tax figures and a reminder |
| First freelance (PFA) offer | An offer as PFA vs employment contract | Contributions jump at 48,600 and 97,200 lei net; the CASS floor; the pension contributions you no longer get automatically | Compare the two offers in net and future terms; set a tax-reserve rule |
| First bonus | A lump sum lands | Buffer first, then goals; the €400/year Pillar III deduction | Split it by rule |
| First tax return | April–May | What Declarația Unică is and when it applies to you | A personal checklist with your figures |

**One moment end to end: the first payslip**

1. On payday Maria gets one nudge: "Your first payslip, explained in 3 minutes."
2. **Predict:** a slider asks how much of her 6,000 lei gross goes to her future. She guesses 300 lei.
3. **Reveal:** she photographs the payslip. **1,500 lei** went to her pension, **285 lei** of it into her own Pillar II account, plus 600 lei to health. Moving the gross slider shows how each part changes.
4. **The fund nobody chose:** how she was assigned, and the funds compared on public data (fees, returns), with no recommendation. Action: check her fund on the Pillar II portal.
5. **Pay yourself first:** her three months of expenses become a buffer target. She picks an amount to move to savings on payday at her own bank (simulated in the prototype).
6. Her "firsts map" marks the payslip done and schedules the next moment, when the monthly inflation figure comes out.

## Business model

Don't sell leads; sell activation. Partners pay for young customers who actually start saving and investing, inside the partner's own app, and the lessons stay neutral.

| Stream | Who pays | What they buy | Pricing (to test) | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partner licence (main) | Banks, neobanks, brokers | The moments embedded in their app (white-label), with the action completed in their product: payday savings rule set up, first state-bond order placed, first recurring investment started | Per monthly active learner, or annual licence plus per-learner fee | Month 6 |
| Employer seats | Companies hiring many under-30s (IT, outsourcing centres, retail chains) | First-job onboarding: payslip, pension fund, PFA vs contract, buffer. Fewer money-stressed new hires | Per employee per year, sold directly or through benefit platforms | Month 3 |
| Standalone app | Nobody | The free app is the funnel and the proof of engagement for partners | Free, no ads | Day 1 |

**Neutrality rules (put them on a slide):**

- Nobody can pay to be recommended. When options are compared, they're ranked by a public formula: return after tax and inflation, minus fees.
- Partners pay the same rate whatever their rank, and the user always chooses where to act.
- No individual investment recommendations: explain instruments and options, never "buy this fund".
- No ads, and no selling user data.

**Why partners pay even when we're neutral:**

- The license is for *their* app. The lesson runs there, and the action (a savings rule, a first state-bond order) happens there.
- They pay for young customers who become active, which their own marketing struggles to buy. Revolut's 5 million Romanian customers and Salt Bank's 1-million target show how hard they compete.
- The industry already funds financial education: BNR, ASF and the banking association signed a schools agreement in 2018.
- Abroad, Zogo built a youth financial-education app on 200+ bank and credit-union partners, reaching \~700,000 mostly under-24 users by 2022.

**Your original idea, kept honestly.** Referral fees per savings account can stay as a minor stream, under the neutrality rules. Don't build the company on them: young savers hold small balances, so each account is worth little to a bank. And getting paid to push the product you just taught about breaks trust with an audience that already distrusts banks.

**Validate before demo day (one call each):**

- [ ] One bank or neobank (Salt Bank, ING, BCR) or broker: would they embed moments and pay per active learner?
- [ ] One HR lead at an IT or outsourcing company: would they pay per seat for first-job onboarding?
- [ ] Ten people under 30: after the payslip moment, did they take the action?

## Go-to-market and scaling

The free app earns trust and proves which moments lead to action. Partners and employers pay for the version inside their world.

**Channels, cheapest first:**

- **Student organizations** at the big universities (ASE, Politehnica, UBB): a "your first job" session before graduation.
- **Employer onboarding:** HR sends the payslip moment with the first salary.
- **Short Romanian videos** cut from the moments themselves ("guess how much of your salary goes to your pension").
- **Inside partner apps**, once a licence is signed.

**Why people come back:** the calendar, not streaks. Payday every month, the monthly inflation figure, monthly state-bond windows, the 25 May tax deadline, and each new "first".

**North-star metric:** actions completed per user per quarter. It's what partners pay for, and it's the proof of "tangible value" the brief asks for.

**Moat:** lesson content is easy to copy. A rules engine that stays correct every year (thresholds, tax rates, deadlines) is not, and neither are partner integrations or data on which moments actually lead to action.

## Why Romania, and how it maps to the brief

**Only in Romania.** Every moment runs on Romanian rules that change yearly:

- the 25% pension contribution with 4.75% routed to Pillar II;
- assignment to a pension fund for those who don't choose one;
- 10% tax on deposit interest vs tax-free Tezaur/Fidelis with monthly windows;
- 3%/6% withheld vs 16% self-declared on investment gains;
- PFA contributions in steps set by the minimum wage.

Copied to another country, the moments are wrong. That's also the scaling path: a new country is a new rule pack, not a new app.

**Why now:** 2026 brought new investment-gain tax rates, inflation of 8.2% against deposits paying 4.4–5.25%, a record year for Fidelis and the PFA wave. Banks and neobanks are competing hard for young customers.

| "new instruments, changing regulations" | Pillar II, Tezaur/Fidelis, 2026 investment taxes, PFA thresholds |

## Regulation, risks and limits

The main risk is drifting from education into advice or selling. The neutrality rules are the mitigation, so make them visible.

- **Investment advice.** Recommending a specific instrument to a specific person is investment advice under MiFID II, licensed by ASF. Explaining instruments and showing options with a public ranking is education. Introducing clients to an investment firm for pay may need tied-agent registration (**verify** with ASF).
- **Credit products.** Any loan comparison or referral for pay may require registration as a credit intermediary (**verify**). Leave credit out of the first version.
- **Tax figures.** Calculating a user's own figures is fine; tax consultancy is a regulated profession. Show the formulas, link to ANAF, never file for the user (**verify** where the line sits).
- **Data.** Payslips and trades are personal data under GDPR: consent per document, minimal storage, deletion on request. Reading bank data later needs a licensed open-banking partner (Smart Fintech holds both BNR licences).
- **Partner conflict.** A bank may not like a lesson showing that state bonds beat its deposit. The licence must say partners can't edit the comparisons; banks that also sell Fidelis have less reason to object.
- **Retention.** Education apps lose users fast. The defence is calendar-driven moments and actions, not content volume. Measure actions, not opens.
- **Accuracy.** The 2026 rules change every year, so the engine needs an annual update and a reviewer who knows the rules. Every number shows its formula and its year.
- **Prototype limits.** Payslips and balances are typed or photographed, partner and bank actions are simulated, and only 2 moments are built.
