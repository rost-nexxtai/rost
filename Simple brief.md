# Prima dată — the simple brief

Oct 28, 2026 · @eddie

## The idea

**Prima dată helps Romanians under 30 understand a money decision at the moment they face it, using their own numbers, and then take one small action.** It works like Brilliant (short, interactive, guess first), but the exercises are your real life, not textbook examples.

**The problem**

- Young Romanians make their first money decisions alone, and Romania has the lowest financial knowledge in the EU (13% score high).
- The rules are Romanian and change every year: where your salary's contributions go, the pension fund you were assigned without choosing, inflation eating your savings, the tax on gains from investing apps.
- Information exists, but nothing explains it *when it matters* and helps you act.

**How it works, in one example**

1. Maria, 23, gets paid. The app sends one notification: "Your payslip, explained in 3 minutes."
2. She **guesses** how much of her 6,000 lei gross goes to her future. She says 300.
3. She **sees** the real answer from her own payslip: 1,500 lei to her pension, 285 lei of it into her own Pillar II account.
4. She **does one thing**: sets 400 lei to move into savings every payday, at her own bank.
5. A week later the app checks: done? That counts as one action completed.

## Moments

A **moment** is one of those 3-minute lessons. It opens because something happened in your money life, not because you're on "lesson 4". Every moment follows the same pattern: **something happens → guess → see your real numbers → one action → a check that you did it.**

**"First" means the first time it's explained to you.** It doesn't have to be the first time it happened. On your 10th payslip, the payslip moment is still "your first payslip explained". After that a moment only comes back when something changes: a raise, a new job, new rules on 1 January.

**How moments are triggered:**

1. **You tell the app.** Three onboarding questions (your situation, your payday, which "firsts" you've already been through), a photo of a payslip or job offer, or a tap: "I opened an investing app".
2. **The calendar.** Fixed dates the app already knows: your payday, the monthly inflation figure, the monthly state-bond windows, the 25 May tax deadline, 1 January rule changes.
3. **Your bank, later.** With your consent and a licensed partner, the app sees real events: salary arrived, savings sitting idle, first transfer to a broker. Inside a partner bank's app this is built in.

**Who it's for: with or without a salary.** Onboarding starts from your situation, and anything you've already been through becomes a "catch up" moment.

| Your situation | Your first moments |
| --- | --- |
| Student, no income | Your first own money (allowance, scholarship, summer job); your first card; a first "buy now, pay later" offer |
| Just graduated | What changes when you stop being a student (e.g. health insurance); your first job offer |
| Employed | Your payslip; the pension fund you were assigned; a savings rule on payday |
| Freelancer (PFA) | Your first paid invoice; the contribution thresholds; your own pension, since nobody withholds it for you |
| Between jobs | Your first month without a salary: how long your money lasts, and what to register |
| Anyone | What inflation does to your savings; your first investing app; your first tax return |

## Who pays

**Users pay nothing.** The money comes from three places:

- **Banks, neobanks and brokers** put the moments inside their own apps and pay per active learner. They want young customers who actually start saving and investing, and the action (like the payday savings rule) happens in their app.
- **Employers** pay per new hire to give young staff the payslip, pension and savings moments in their first weeks.
- **Referrals, ranked neutrally.** At the end of a savings moment the app shows guaranteed options (deposits and state bonds) ranked by what you keep after tax. Partner banks pay a fee when you open and fund an account, but the order never changes and partners are labelled. This is the smallest stream: young savers hold small balances.

**One rule we never break:** nobody can pay to be recommended. When options are compared, they're ranked by a public formula, the same for every partner.
